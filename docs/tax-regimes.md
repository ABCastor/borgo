# Newcomer tax regimes

Many countries tax people who move there to work less than everyone else, for a few years. Who you are, where you come from and what you do decide whether you qualify. This page lists the regimes the money model knows, as of October 2026, and how they reach the take-home figure.

## How it works

- Each regime is a record in [`src/tax/regimes.ts`](https://github.com/ABCastor/borgo/blob/main/src/tax/regimes.ts): conditions, effect, duration, sources and caveats. Country tax modules stay as they are; a regime reaches them only as the generic tax tweak they already accept.
- **Off unless you switch it on.** `IncomePlan.regimes` holds the regime you chose per country (`{ "NL": "nl-expat" }`). A regime you merely qualify for changes nothing. Anyone else in the household chooses their own in `Person.regimes` (two Dutch employees can each have the 30% ruling). A chosen regime is checked against that person's facts (`chosenRegime`): *established* when every condition is met, a *scenario* while some are yours to confirm (counted, labelled, with the conditions listed), and not counted when a fact fails one. Each person's pay takes only their own.
- **Eligibility.** `eligibleRegimes(country, facts)` gives every regime of a country a verdict per condition: *met*, *not met*, or *confirm* (yours to answer). The facts are few, set per person in `Person.newcomer`: age, children under 18 moving with you, years since you were last tax resident in each country, and yes/no answers to conditions no dataset can check (profession, degree, how you were hired). Passports come from the household, pay from the income plan. A missing fact never fails a condition; it leaves it for you to confirm.
- **Effect.** `regimeTweak(regime, gross)` turns a regime into a tweak for that gross pay. A tax-free part becomes a *taxable share* (it reaches every income-tax layer, never social contributions); a flat rate replaces the national schedule, or, with `flatRateScope: "all"`, every income-tax layer (regional, municipal, church), as in Spain, Denmark and Finland. Caps and salary floors are applied to the gross, which is why the share is worked out per city. Your own tweak fields win over the regime's. A pension deduction comes off the taxed share in full. Spain's special regime taxes the whole of gross pay (art. 93 LIRPF: no social security deduction, no reductions); Finland's key-employee tax also replaces the health insurance contributions and the Yle tax; Italy's cuneo fiscale still reads the exempt half as income (L. 207/2024 art. 1 c. 9).

## Countries with nothing for newcomers' pay

| Country | What there is |
|---|---|
| Germany, Czechia, Estonia | No regime: pay is taxed on the normal schedule. |
| Hungary | No regime tied to moving; its exemptions (under 25, mothers of two or more) apply to everyone. |
| Switzerland | No lower rate; posted executives and specialists may deduct some extra costs for up to five years (Expatriates Ordinance). Not modelled. |
| United Kingdom | No regime for UK pay. The FIG regime exempts **foreign** income and gains for 4 years after 10 years away ([HMRC HS266](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/foreign-income-and-gains-fig-regime-self-assessment-helpsheet-hs266/hs266-foreign-income-and-gains-fig-regime-2026)); Overseas Workday Relief covers pay for work done abroad. |
| United States | None, federal or state. |
| Canada | None federally; Quebec has tax holidays for some foreign researchers and experts (not modelled). |
| Australia | None for Australian pay; temporary residents are spared tax on most foreign income. |
| Japan | None for Japanese pay; non-permanent residents are spared tax on foreign income not paid into Japan. |
| Singapore | None since the Not Ordinarily Resident scheme ended after the 2020 year of assessment. |
| United Arab Emirates | No personal income tax at all. |

## What to know before relying on it

- Figures describe 2026. Coming changes already announced: the Netherlands goes to 27% from 2027 for people who started from 2024 (higher salary threshold from 2027 for those who started from 2025); Sweden has a bill for 30% from 2027 that also opens the relief to Swedish citizens (not law when checked); Denmark's threshold rises to DKK 67,300 a month in 2027 (skat.dk's Danish page; the 2026 figure is from its English page).
- Italy's 60% tier is for a child under 18 only. The South Italy extra belonged to the old 2019 regime and no longer applies to people moving from 2024.
- Social contributions are unchanged by every regime here, though in the Netherlands, Belgium and Sweden the tax-free part is also free of some contributions; country models still charge them on the whole gross, so take-home is slightly understated there.
- Belgium, Greece, Austria and Denmark are read from the official texts as of 27 October 2026. Greece's article 5C comes from Law 4758/2020; Law 5222/2025 removed its "newly created job" requirement from July 2025.
- Country modules must send every sub-national income-tax layer through `taxedShare()` in `src/tax/common.ts`, or a flat rate with scope "all" will not remove it. Belgium's regional and municipal surcharges are percentages of the federal tax rather than taxes on a base, so they follow a taxable share through `incomeTax()` and the module drops them itself under scope "all".
