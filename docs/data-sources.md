# Data sources

Every figure on the site comes from one of the sources in the registry (`registry/<source>.json`: what it measures, licence, how it is fetched, who vetted it) and carries its source, period, licence and geographic level. Any place can be fetched on demand on the person's side (`docs/architecture.md`); this page describes the committed snapshot for the 57 seed cities that the public pages show. Each snapshot source is one script in `pipeline/sources/` that writes `data/observations/<source>.json` (or the folder its licence requires, below), using the same fetcher a person runs where one exists; `npm run data` (`node pipeline/build-data.ts`) then checks every observation against the registry and its rights, and assembles the published files and their licence manifest.

Coverage is out of the 57 cities in `data/cities.json`. Level says how local a figure is: **city** (the city itself, or a fixed circle around its centre), **country** (a national figure standing in for the city, marked as approximate on the site).

## Known caveats

- **Temperature** is air temperature 2 m above the ground in the shade; "feels like" (apparent temperature) adds humidity, wind and sun and is a separate metric. Both come from the ERA5 reanalysis grid (about 25 km), which smooths coasts and hills.
- **Sunshine** is measured at weather stations (WMO 1991-2020 normals within 50 km, else Meteostat within 80 km, both within 500 m of height); the note names the station and its distance. Modelled sunshine read 32-92% high against stations and is not used. San Francisco, Los Angeles, New York, Bologna and Cambridge have no station within the rules.

- **OpenStreetMap counts** measure mapping as well as reality. Station tagging varies (Dubai's metro barely registers as stations), and some regional parks are mostly farmland (Milan's Parco Agricolo Sud lifts its green share).
- **Distance to the sea** ignores lakes and four tidal rivers that Natural Earth draws as coast far inland (Elbe, Scheldt, St Lawrence, Hudson; boxes in `pipeline/sources/geography.ts`). The Thames is kept, so London reads 36 km.
- **The world map's land** shows months whose average afternoon high is in your range, from NASA POWER's monthly climatology (MERRA-2, 2001-2025) averaged over each tile of about 150 km. It is air temperature only, and a tile that holds mountains reads cooler than a city in its valley (Turin's tile has July at 24 °C; the city's own figure is near 30). The cities on the map keep their own nice days. Details in `docs/architecture.md`, section 6.1.
- **Distance to the mountains** means ground 1,000 m above the city centre, read from about 430 m pixels with one-pixel spikes filtered out; 300 means none within 300 km (Dublin's Wicklow hills, under 1,000 m, do not count).
- **Eurostat Urban Audit** figures are each city's latest report, from 2011 to 2025; older than 2010 is dropped. UK cities stop around 2018.
- **M-Lab internet speeds** are from 2023, the last full year M-Lab aggregated; single-stream tests read lower than commercial ones.
- **Same-sex couples in law** is curated by hand from the statutes, reviewed 2026-10-27. Poland and Japan had changes in progress; check them before relying on the figure.
- **Adults with a degree** comes from the most local source there is: the city's own Urban Audit figure (2014 or later), else its NUTS 2 region from the EU Labour Force Survey (one survey, latest year, the city itself for Berlin, Hamburg, Vienna, Prague, Budapest and Brussels), else the World Bank's national figure, which counts ages 25 and over rather than 25 to 64. A region with countryside around its city reads lower than the city (Piemonte 21%, Turin 26%).
- **Developers on GitHub** is a count of profiles whose location names the city, read on the day of the snapshot, over the GeoNames population. Most populations are the city proper, a few the metropolitan area (Sydney, Melbourne), which lowers their rate. A city whose name a large place elsewhere shares gets no figure when profiles naming a country show the other place is common (Cambridge); Barcelona and Valencia keep theirs, with the share of the other place in the note.
- **Everyday needs on foot** and **cycle network** come from OpenStreetMap (ODbL, kept in `data/observations-odbl/osm-walk-cycle.json`). The walking test is straight-line and counts parks from their nearest edge; mapping gaps lower a city's figure, and when one need is missing for more than half the streets the map is taken to be thin and no figure is given (Dubai, where pharmacies are barely mapped). Copenhagen's cycle tracks are mostly tagged on its roads (463 of 967 km), which is why they are counted.
- **Walking or cycling to work** needs both shares from the same year, 2010 or later, so Copenhagen and Amsterdam (last reported 2003) have none; the US figure leaves people working from home out, like the Urban Audit.
- **Out of the dense city** measures to the edge of the GHSL urban centre (1 km squares with at least 1,500 residents per km²): suburbs, farmland, hills or a large park lie beyond, not always open country. Directions that meet the sea or a lake within 3 km of the edge are dropped. Dubai's centre point falls in a gap inside its urban centre and has no figure.

## Computed in the engine, not stored

| Line | How | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Winter daylight | Day length on the shortest day, from latitude (`src/core/sun.ts`) | city coordinates |
| Time difference from home | Clock gap in mid-January and mid-July, the short way round the day, averaged (`src/core/geo.ts`) | each city's IANA time zone (from GeoNames) and the person's home city |
| Flight home | Great-circle distance at 830 km/h plus 30 minutes, nonstop (`src/core/geo.ts`) | coordinates and the person's home city |

## Licences: which file a figure is published in, and the gate

A source may enter the published data only when its registry entry carries a `rights` block: a licence id from the catalogue in `src/registry/licences.ts`, the exact edition read, the terms page and the date it was read, the attribution its figures need, and any notice its terms require. A link-out source cannot have a rights block, so nothing from it can be exported. The licence decides the file, and `pipeline/build-data.ts` refuses to build when a figure's source has no rights block, an unknown licence, or sits in another folder than its licence requires (`test/registry.test.ts` plants an unknown licence and checks that the build fails).

| Folder | Published file | Terms | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| `data/observations/` | `data/dataset.json` | CC BY 4.0 for Borgo's compilation; each figure keeps its source's terms and attribution | every source whose licence allows a CC BY compilation with attribution only (public domain, CC0, CC BY, OGL, Licence Ouverte, OECD data terms, ECB, Unicode, MIT, Copernicus via Open-Meteo, NCEI) |
| `data/observations-odbl/` | `data/dataset-odbl.json` | ODbL 1.0 | OpenStreetMap |
| `data/observations-who/` | `data/dataset-who.json` | WHO terms for data compilations | WHO Ambient Air Quality Database |
| `data/observations-cmhc/` | `data/dataset-cmhc.json` | CMHC Licence Agreement for the Use of Data | CMHC Rental Market Survey |

`public/data/licences.json` is the manifest of the build: for every source actually exported, its edition, licence, terms page and date read, attribution, notices, the file holding its figures, how many figures, which metrics and their retrieval dates. The app, the CLI, the MCP server and the static pages merge the files; the static JSON API keeps them apart (`api/city/<id>.json` holds the CC BY part, `api/<part>/city/<id>.json` the others, each with its notice). The Data page, `/sources/` and the Credits section of every city page show each source's required attribution and notices.

Decisions, with the terms read on 2026-11-05:

- **OpenStreetMap (ODbL 1.0)** is share-alike: figures derived from it are a derived database under the ODbL, published only in `dataset-odbl.json`. Wherever they are shown: "© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL".
- **WHO Ambient Air Quality Database, Version 8.0 (June 2026)** is published on who.int under WHO's terms for data compilations: use for public health purposes, attribution to WHO and the countries that provided the data, minimal alteration, no endorsement. It is not CC BY, so its values stay unaltered in their own file, each note naming the row's data provider. WHO advises against ranking cities with it; the credit line says so.
- **WMO 1991-2020 normals (NCEI accession 0253808, version 6.6)**: NCEI attaches no licence; its record asks for the citation `WMO Member Nations (2023), doi:10.25921/800j-vn07` and disclaims liability. The WMO Unified Data Policy (Resolution 1, Cg-Ext 2021) defines free and unrestricted exchange as no conditions on use and treats attribution as encouraged. The citation is shown wherever the figures are.
- **Open-Meteo** API data are CC BY 4.0 and ask for "Weather data by Open-Meteo.com" with a link; ERA5 and CAMS outputs also carry the Copernicus notice ("Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information", and the same for the Atmosphere Monitoring Service) and the statement that neither the European Commission nor ECMWF is responsible for their use. Open-Meteo's free API is for non-commercial use; this project is non-commercial.
- **Terrain tiles** at zoom 8 are built from SRTM, GMTED2010 (above 60° N), ETOPO1 and, in Canada, NRCAN: public domain US data and the Open Government Licence – Canada. Only derived distances are stored; the credit names each.
- **OECD** data (childcare costs, parental leave, well-being) are under the OECD's terms for data, not CC BY: any use with the OECD citation, and the acknowledgement requirement passed on to anyone the data is shared with.
- **World Bank** indicators: each indicator's own metadata says CC BY-4.0 (PA.NUS.PRVT.PP, PA.NUS.FCRF, VC.IHR.PSRC.P5, SP.DYN.LE00.IN, SH.XPD.OOPC.PC.CD, SH.MED.PHYS.ZS, SL.UEM.TOTL.ZS, GOV_WGI_CC.EST); none carries a third-party exception.
- **Eurostat** reuse is authorised with the source acknowledged and changes stated (Commission Decision 2011/833/EU); the credit says Borgo keeps each city's latest value.
- **Meteostat** is CC BY 4.0 for data it redistributes, with credit to Meteostat and its data providers. **NASA POWER** data are CC0 under NASA's data use guidance; POWER asks every work product to name the project, service version and date read. **ParlGov 2024** is CC0 (Harvard Dataverse record). **CMHC** rents come through a Statistics Canada table whose note says they are provided under CMHC's licence (revocable, recipients bound by it), not the Statistics Canada Open Licence, so they have their own file.
- **Founders Running Club** publishes no data licence. Only the fact of a chapter's presence, its link and the date read are stored; if they object, the source is dropped and the line becomes a personal one.
- **GitHub** grants no data licence; its Acceptable Use Policies allow public, non-personal information to be used for research that is published openly. Only total counts of profiles are kept, never an account or any profile data.
- **GHSL** urban centres (GHS-UCDB R2024A) are CC BY 4.0: credit "GHS-UCDB R2024A, European Commission, Joint Research Centre", doi:10.2905/1a338be6-7eaf-480c-9664-3a8ade88cbcd.

## Rent: what each source measures

Rent is the largest cost in the money comparison, and official sources measure different things. Each figure's note and its source's definition in the registry say which of these it is.

| Source | Cities | What it measures | Asking or sitting | Utilities |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ONS Price Index of Private Rents | London, Cambridge, Manchester, Bristol | average rent, one and three bedrooms | every private tenancy, new and existing | excluded |
| CSO / RTB RIA02 | Dublin, Cork | average rent of tenancies registered that year | new tenancies | excluded |
| US Census ACS, table B25031 | the nine US cities | median gross rent by bedrooms | every tenancy | included (gross rent) |
| CMHC Rental Market Survey | Toronto, Vancouver, Montréal | average rent of purpose-built rental units by bedrooms, October | every tenancy | as each lease includes them |
| Statistics Norway, table 09895 | Oslo (Oslo and Bærum) | average monthly rent, 2 and 4 rooms | every tenancy | as surveyed |
| Statistics Sweden, BO0406 | Stockholm | median annual rent / 12, 2 and 4+ rooms | every tenancy (negotiated rents) | excluded unless in the rent |
| Statistics Finland, table 15fc | Helsinki | median monthly rent of non-subsidised two-room flats | every tenancy | as in the rent |
| Carte des loyers 2025 | Paris, Lyon | modelled asking rent per m² × 50 m² (1-2 rooms) or 90 m² (3+ rooms) | asking (adverts) | charges included |

Where a source gives rent per square metre, a one-bedroom home is 50 m² and a three-bedroom home 90 m² (`pipeline/rent.ts`), the same for every such source; the note gives the rate and the size. Rooms counted without the kitchen (Norway, Sweden, Finland): 2 rooms is one bedroom, 4 rooms three. An asking rent reads higher than a sitting rent for the same home, and a gross rent higher than a net one, so compare across countries with that in mind.

Checked and not used yet: NSW's Rent and Sales Report (new bonds, Greater Sydney) is published with "licence specified by agency" and no licence could be read; Victoria's rental report files did not download; Spain's SERPAVI municipal medians (tax data, every tenancy) are available, but the ministry's reuse terms could not be read; Germany (Zensus 2022), Switzerland (BFS), Austria, the Netherlands, Portugal, Italy, Japan and Singapore are not yet checked.
